Routing Specification

Input: an ordered list of role names (from `--path`) and, after each role runs, its result `{handoff, output_document, review_items}`, where each review item is `{section, verdict}` and verdict is exactly "approve" or "reject". Run the roles in the given order, one at a time, passing each role only the scoped handoff from the previous role (task, from_role, summary, decisions, citation_list, written_entry_ids, output_document) and never its session history. After the last role, collect the results of every role whose name starts with "reviewer". If two or more reviewers ran, set `escalated_to_human = true` when any of these hold, otherwise `false`: (a) a reviewer returned no review items or a verdict other than "approve"/"reject"; (b) the reviewers' overall verdicts differ, where a reviewer's overall verdict is "reject" if any of its items is "reject", otherwise "approve"; (c) the same section, after normalizing its name (lowercase, hyphens and spaces to single underscores), has both an "approve" and a "reject" verdict. Implemented in `scripts/reviewer_conflict_deterministic.py`. Output a transcript with `expected_path`, `events`, `duration_seconds`, `token_cost`, and `escalated_to_human`. Known edge cases: (1) reviewers that disagree under different section names (e.g. `implementation` vs `action_planning`) — caught by rule (b) and tested in `eval/test_reviewer_conflict.py`; (2) review passes but tests fail — no rule currently routes back to the Implementer.
